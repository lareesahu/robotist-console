From demo to decision

A demo is not a night shift.

A robot can look good on a polished showroom floor. We turn that into a written plan for your site: which aisles, which window, what the floor has to look like at shift start, and who is responsible when it is not.

Step 1

Walk the warehouse

Floor plans, floor condition, aisle widths, racking layout, debris, dock traffic, shift times, power outlets, network coverage and the safety systems involved.

Step 2

Write the job down

Which areas, which hours, what the result has to look like, what staff still do and what the robot will not do — agreed before a model is picked.

Step 3

Test it if practical

One route or one task, measured against the agreed numbers, including disruption to people and how often staff had to step in.

Step 4

Put it in the proposal

The chosen system, the test results, the full cost, what your warehouse has to provide, staff training, how we will judge the result and who fixes it.

What a proposal should show

Your floor is the test, not the showroom.

  • Floor cleaned before shift start
  • Routes completed
  • Safety incidents and near misses
  • Times staff step in
  • Cost per night
  • Who handles repairs

Before you buy

Questions worth asking.

Should it clean during the shift or between shifts?

Between shifts is easier and safer: an empty floor, a fixed window, a measurable result. Cleaning while pickers are working is possible but needs the route, the safety zones and the speed agreed with your team first.

Can it handle dust, tape and packing debris?

A scrubber is built for dust and general floor dirt, not for wrapping, strapping or spilled product. If debris collects on the floor, someone clears it first — the site check agrees who and when.

What happens when pallets block the route?

The machine maps the floor, so a changed layout needs a rescan or an agreed no-go zone. A warehouse that rearranges weekly needs that routine written into the plan rather than discovered on the first night.

Can a quadruped patrol at night?

Yes as a project: route, network coverage through the racking, lighting, doors, a safety review and someone to read what the sensors record. The hardware is in our catalogue; the patrol is designed with you.

Do you supply forklifts, AMRs or conveyor systems?

No. Those are bought per project with a systems integrator, because they need a link into your warehouse management system and a safety case. We will tell you who needs to be involved rather than sell around it.

Can we try one before buying?

Yes. We start with a review of your site and agree what will be measured. A trial is included when it is practical and agreed in scope, and the results go into the proposal.
