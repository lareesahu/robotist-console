Why that happens

LOOI's companion app wasn't built to work with a hotel's own information and rules.

The phone inside LOOI runs its companion app: one model, one personality, memory tied to a consumer account. The app provides no way to add hotel-specific knowledge or set property-level rules.

Nothing you can change at account level affects what the robot says or decides. Changing that means owning the software layer behind it, while keeping the robot itself.

What this will not do

Scope and limitations.

It won't take bookings

It answers guests and routes them to the right desk. Nothing in this scope writes into Marriott's systems: no reservation changes, no inventory moves, and no bookings taken on a guest's behalf.

It won't recognise guests

No name lookups, no stay history, and no memory of a guest between visits. Guest identification is excluded from this pilot; any future use would need your explicit approval.

It isn't enterprise hardware

LOOI is a consumer device, without fleet management or a security-patching commitment. We recommend keeping the first deployment to this pilot; a wider rollout may need a different platform, and we'll say so if it does.

One robot, one property, one workflow

Additional properties or workflows are designed and quoted separately.

It won't invent answers

Where approved material doesn't cover a question, the robot says it can't answer and directs the guest to staff.

In the property

Proposed pilot use.

Lobby, concierge and one guest floor

Greeting guests and pointing them the right way, routing requests at concierge, and an in-room companion trial on one floor. Supported languages and answers are agreed during validation.

Guest data

The pilot will not continuously record audio, use facial recognition, or retain guest identity across stays. Before deployment we document data retention, access controls and the subprocessors involved, for approval by your security team.
